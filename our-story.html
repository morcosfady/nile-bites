Our Story

Heritage

Everything here has a source

Nothing at Pharaoh's Bites was invented in a test kitchen. Every dish came from a house, a village or a market stall, and someone in this family can tell you whose. What follows is how a bakery window became a dining room — and why nothing about the food changed along the way.

Pharaoh's Bites timeline

1974

The window on Abou El Feda

Fatma Farid sells her gold bangles for a second-hand stone oven and starts selling feteer meshaltet through a window to the street. Two items. No sign above the door.

1981

The book is written down

Her son Farid insists the recipes are recorded before they are lost. Forty pages, in her hand, with weights measured in fistfuls and cups. It is still the reference today.

1989

Breakfast begins

Neighbours ask for foul with their bread. A single copper qidra goes on at midnight, and by morning there is a queue. The Egyptian breakfast table is born.

1996

The second oven

A dedicated bakery oven is built so bread and pastry never compete for the fire. Amira, sixteen, is given the four a.m. shift. She has kept it ever since.

2004

A dining room, finally

The building next door is taken over and twenty-two covers are laid. The window stays open for the street — it still is, every morning at half past five.

2009

Hossam comes home

After six years in Lyon, the grandson returns to learn the book properly. He spends the first eleven months doing nothing but stretching dough.

2016

The Pharaoh's Table

A seven-course tasting menu traces the Egyptian day from breakfast to sweet. It sells out for eight months straight and has never come off the menu.

2021

The room you sit in now

A full restoration in sandstone, brass and dark timber — Pharaonic proportion without a single pyramid. Ninety covers. The same forty pages.

Today

Third generation, first light

Eighteen thousand loaves a month, a hundred and twenty dishes, and an oven that has not been allowed to go cold in fifty-one years.

Handcrafted

Thirty folds, and not one of them optional

Feteer meshaltet is a test. The dough is rested, oiled, and then thrown — not rolled — until it is thin enough to read through. It is folded, oiled and folded again, thirty times, and the moment it is rushed you can taste it.

01

Rest

Four hours minimum. The gluten has to forget it was ever kneaded.

02

Throw

Stretched by hand in the air until light passes through. No pin, ever.

03

Fold

Clarified butter between every layer, thirty times, always the same direction.

04

Fire

Nine minutes on bare stone at four hundred degrees. It shatters, or it failed.

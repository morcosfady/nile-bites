Who We Are

Pharaoh's Bites is a family
before it is a restaurant

In 1974 my grandmother Fatma sold her gold bangles to buy a second-hand oven and began selling feteer from a window on Abou El Feda Street. She had no menu. She had a book — forty pages of recipes in her own hand, some inherited from her mother in Assiut, some traded with neighbours over decades.

That book is still in the kitchen. It is stained, it is taped at the spine, and it is the final word on every dish we serve. When a supplier offers us a shortcut, we check the book. When a chef suggests an improvement, we check the book. It has not lost an argument yet.

Today the third generation runs the pass. The room is more beautiful than the window on Abou El Feda ever was. The food is exactly the same.

What We Stand On

Six promises, kept daily

None of these are marketing. Each one costs us money, time or sleep — which is precisely why they are worth writing down.

Fresh Ingredients

We buy from the same four families we bought from in the eighties. Vegetables arrive at dawn, herbs twice a day, and nothing is frozen — not once, not ever.

Traditional Recipes

Forty pages, three generations, zero substitutions. If a dish takes nine hours it takes nine hours, and we start it the night before.

Daily Handmade Baking

The oven is lit at four in the morning. Bread every twenty minutes, feteer stretched to order, pastry laminated by hand over three days.

Warm Hospitality

Nobody is rushed off a table at Pharaoh's Bites. Sit for four hours if you like. The tea is refilled without asking and the bread keeps arriving.

Authentic Flavours

We grind our own cumin and coriander weekly, press our own tahini, and cure our own basterma in fenugreek. The taste has a source.

Premium Quality

First-press olive oil, buffalo milk from the Delta, Aleppo pistachio, Siwa dates and salt. We would rather raise the price than lower the grade.
